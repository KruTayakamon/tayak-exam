R analytical-article 2|The Rise and Limits of the Gig Economy
Over the past decade, app-based work, from food delivery to freelance design, has become ubiquitous in Thai cities. Supporters point to its flexibility: workers can choose when and how much to work. For students and parents with young children, this is a genuine advantage.

However, the flexibility comes at a cost. Most gig workers have no paid leave, no health insurance from their employer, and no guaranteed income. A rainy week or a change in the app's pay rates can halve their earnings. This risk is not incidental; it is inherent in a model that treats workers as independent contractors.

The overarching question, then, is not whether the gig economy should exist, but how its benefits can be preserved while its risks are shared more fairly. Some countries now require platforms to contribute to accident insurance, a modest but meaningful first step.
