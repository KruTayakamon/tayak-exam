R book-review 2|Review: "Teaching in Plain Words" by Dr Anan T.
Few books on education manage to be both rigorous and readable. This one comes close. Dr Anan argues that teachers too often hide behind jargon, and he practises what he preaches: his prose is plain, his chapters short and his examples drawn from real classrooms in Thailand and Laos.

The strongest section deals with feedback. Anan shows that pithy, specific comments ("Your second paragraph needs evidence") help students far more than long, general ones. Teachers will find dozens of usable examples here.

If the book has a weakness, it is that it occasionally becomes too terse. The chapter on assessment, for instance, raises important questions but answers them in just six pages. A longer treatment would have been welcome. Even so, this is a refreshing and useful critique of how we talk about teaching.
