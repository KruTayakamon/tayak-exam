R workplace-communication 2|Why Clear Messages Save Money
A recent survey of 500 office workers found that unclear emails cost the average employee almost two hours a week. Most of this time was spent asking for clarification or correcting work that had been done wrongly.

The most common problem was not grammar but structure. Many messages buried the key request in the third or fourth paragraph, so readers missed it. Others relied on implicit assumptions, such as "the usual format" or "the same as last time", which new staff could not interpret.

Communication trainers suggest a simple rule: state the action and the deadline in the first sentence, then give the background. They also recommend avoiding ambiguous words like "soon" and "shortly". As one trainer put it, "If you mean Friday at noon, write Friday at noon."

-slow internet connections|ในบทไม่ได้พูดถึงอินเทอร์เน็ต
