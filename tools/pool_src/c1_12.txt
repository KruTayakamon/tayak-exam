R long-analytical 2|The Hidden Costs of Cheap Fashion
Clothes have never been cheaper. A T-shirt that cost 300 baht a decade ago can now be bought online for less than 100. For consumers on tight budgets, this is a tangible benefit, and critics who simply tell people to "buy less" often ignore this reality.

Yet low prices conceal costs that are paid elsewhere. Garment workers in some countries earn wages that barely cover rent and food. Synthetic fabrics release tiny plastic fibres when washed, and much of the clothing discarded each year ends up in landfill or is burned.

There is no simple solution. Banning cheap clothing would hurt low-income families most. However, a plethora of smaller measures, such as repair services, clothing swaps, clearer labelling and minimum wage agreements across supply chains, could together reduce the damage. The impetus for change, the writer argues, must come from companies and governments, not only from individual shoppers.
