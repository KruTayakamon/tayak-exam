R lifelong-learning 2|Never Too Late: Learning at Sixty
When Khun Prasert retired at sixty, he expected to spend his time gardening. Instead, he enrolled in an online course on Thai history. "I was always inquisitive," he says, "but work never left me time to follow my interests."

He admits the start was hard. The video platform was unfamiliar, and he sometimes felt embarrassed asking younger classmates for help. But he discovered that his life experience was valuable in discussions, and several classmates said his comments made the topics feel more authentic.

Two years later, Prasert has completed four courses and now volunteers at a local museum, giving tours to school groups. Research supports his experience: older adults who keep learning tend to report better memory and a stronger sense of purpose, although scientists caution that more active people may simply be more likely to study in the first place.
