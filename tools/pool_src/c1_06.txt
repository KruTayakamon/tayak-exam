R research-analysis 2|A Closer Look at the "Morning Study" Claim
A popular article recently claimed that people who study in the morning remember 40 per cent more than those who study at night. The claim sounds plausible, but a closer look at the original research suggests caution.

First, the study involved only 36 university students, all from one faculty. Second, the morning group slept on average one hour longer than the evening group, so sleep, not time of day, may explain the difference. Third, the 40 per cent figure came from a single vocabulary test given the following day.

None of this means the finding is wrong. It would appear, however, that the article overstated a tentative result. Until larger studies corroborate it, readers should treat the claim as an interesting possibility rather than established fact.

-it was not measured|มีการวัดและรายงาน
