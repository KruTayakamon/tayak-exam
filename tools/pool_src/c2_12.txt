R long-academic 2|Rethinking Teacher Pay
Debates about teacher remuneration often focus on a single question: are teachers paid enough? The answer, in most countries, is probably no. Yet a narrower focus on salary may miss what research suggests matters equally to teacher retention: working conditions, professional respect and opportunities to grow.

A survey of teachers who left the profession found that fewer than a third named pay as their main reason. More common were workload, administrative paperwork and a sense that their expertise was not valued. Conversely, teachers who stayed often cited supportive leadership and collaborative colleagues.

This does not mean that pay is unimportant. Low salaries make it harder to attract candidates of high calibre in the first place. But raising pay without improving conditions risks treating the symptom rather than the cause. A more holistic approach would combine fair remuneration with reduced paperwork and genuine career pathways.
